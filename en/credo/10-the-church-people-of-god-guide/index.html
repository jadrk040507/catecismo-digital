Catechist's Guide — The Church, People of God

Catechist's Guide

For class preparation and delivery. For the catechist's use only.

Class Summary

Estimated duration: 60 minutes

Central theme: The Church is the People of God, the Body of Christ, and the Temple of the Holy Spirit, called to be one, holy, catholic, and apostolic.

Objectives:

  • Understand that the Church is not a building, but the people God has gathered from the beginning of the world.
  • Know the biblical images of the Church: People of God, Body of Christ, Temple of the Holy Spirit.
  • Interiorize the meaning of the four marks of the Church: one, holy, catholic, and apostolic.
  • Discover one’s own place in the Church as members of the Body of Christ.
  • Love the Church as Christ loves her, despite the wounds of her members.

Key verse: «But you are a chosen race, a royal priesthood, a holy nation, God’s own people, that you may declare the wonderful deeds of him who called you out of darkness into his marvelous light» (1 Pet 2:9)

Catechism references: CCC 751-870

Suggested Class Structure

Section Duration What the catechist does
1. Big Question (Story of Saint Augustine) 10 min Tell the story of Saint Augustine and his search. Ask: «What is the Church to you?»
2. The Church in God’s plan 8 min Explain «ekklesía» and the biblical images of the Church. Tree activity for children.
3. People of God / Body of Christ 12 min 1 Pet 2:9-10; 1 Cor 12:12-13. Explain baptismal priesthood and diversity of gifts. Reflection on community.
4. One, Holy, Catholic, and Apostolic 12 min Explain the four marks. Use Eph 4:4-6 and Mt 16:18-19. Connect with participants’ experience.
5. For Reflection 8 min Invite small groups to share one of the 5 reflection questions, then plenary.
6. Closing: Key Takeaway + Prayer 5 min Read the Key Takeaway. Pray the workbook prayer together.
7. Week assignment 5 min Hand out the workbook. Invite them to research their parish and write the letter to a persecuted Christian.

Notes for the Catechist

The story of Saint Augustine

It is important that participants understand that Augustine’s conversion was not an individual matter. He sought truth through intellectual paths, but upon finding Christ he understood that faith needs a body, a people, a community. Saint Ambrose was key in his journey. The Church is not an obstacle to faith; it is the place where faith is lived and grows.

The meaning of «ekklesía»

The Greek word ekklesía means «assembly called together.» It is not a gathering that people organize on their own; it is God who calls. The Church does not begin with us; it begins with God. That changes the perspective: we are not the ones who «make» the Church; God calls it together and we respond.

The four marks of the Church

It is crucial to distinguish between the Church as God wills it (one, holy, catholic, apostolic) and the Church as we see it with its human limitations. The marks are not our merits; they are gifts from Christ. The holiness of the Church does not come from our perfection, but from the presence of Christ and the Holy Spirit. It is important to address this honestly, without hiding the Church’s wounds.

The Church and scandal

It is almost certain that someone will raise the scandals in the Church. Do not avoid them. Acknowledge the pain and shame with sincerity. But help distinguish: the Church is holy in her Head (Christ) and in the sacraments, even though her members are sinners. The fact that the Church continues to exist despite her sinners is, paradoxically, a sign that Christ sustains her.

The baptismal priesthood

Many Catholics do not know about the baptismal priesthood. Explain it clearly: we are not «passive laypeople» who just go to Mass. Every baptized person shares in the priesthood of Christ and offers their life as spiritual worship. That means your work, your studies, your family, your service to others — all of it can be an offering to God.

Age Adaptation

🌱 Seed (ages 7-12)

Use the image of the body: what would happen if a hand said «I don’t need the body»? Play a game where each child represents a part of the body and they have to work together to accomplish something. Activities: draw the Church as a house with many rooms, color a body with different members, memorize 1 Pet 2:9 with hand signs.

🌿 Sprouts (ages 13-17)

Connect with their experience of belonging: do they feel part of something bigger? A team, a band, a family? The Church is like a community where each person has a unique gift. Talk about young saints (Saint John Bosco, Saint Thérèse, Blessed Carlo Acutis) as models of active belonging. The scandal in the Church is a real topic at this age; do not avoid it.

🪴 Root (adults new to the faith)

Many adults come with negative experiences of the Church: routine, hypocrisy, scandals. Be honest: acknowledge the wounds. But help them see the difference between the Church as a human institution and the Church as a mystery of faith. Share testimonies of vibrant communities, of saints who transformed the Church from within.

🌳 Tree (formed adults)

Go deeper into the ecclesiology of Vatican II: Lumen Gentium, the Church as universal sacrament of salvation, the relationship between the ministerial priesthood and the baptismal priesthood. You can quote the Church Fathers (Saint Cyprian: «He cannot have God for his Father who does not have the Church for his mother»). Address ecumenism and the relationship with other Christian confessions.

Difficult Questions

«If the Church is holy, why is there so much scandal and sin in it?»

This is perhaps the most painful question. The Catechism responds that the Church is «chaste and a sinner» at the same time: holy in her Head (Christ) and in the sacraments, but in need of purification in her members (CCC 823-829). The sins of Christians —including pastors— do not destroy the holiness of the Church, but they wound it gravely. Christ never abandons his Church; he purifies it continually. As Saint Augustine said: the Church is holy even though it has sinners in its midst, because Christ sanctifies it.

«Why do I need the Church? Isn’t it enough to believe in God my own way?»

The Christian faith is not individualistic. From the beginning, God wanted to gather a people, not save isolated individuals. Jesus did not leave a book; he left a community with structure and authority. Saint Paul says we are «members one of another» (Rom 12:5). The Church is the Body of Christ: you cannot separate the head from the body. Believing «your own way» is like wanting to have the head without the body. Moreover, the sacraments —which are the ordinary means of grace— need a community to be celebrated.

«What does it mean that the Church is catholic? Isn’t that exclusionary?»

Catholic means «universal,» not «exclusionary.» The Church is catholic because Christ sent her to all peoples, not to a single group (Mk 16:15). It is the opposite of exclusionary: it is open to all cultures, languages, and peoples. Catholicity means that in every local community —your parish— the whole Church is present. It is not that there are many Churches; it is one Church present throughout the world (CCC 830-835).

«Why do Catholics obey the Pope? Isn’t that anti-democratic?»

The Pope is not a political leader; he is the successor of Saint Peter, to whom Christ entrusted the mission of confirming his brothers in the faith (Lk 22:32). The Pope’s authority is a service: to guarantee the unity of the Church and safeguard the apostolic faith. It is not anti-democratic; it is a charism of service. The Church also has structures of participation: councils, synods, parish councils. The Pope’s authority does not eliminate the responsibility of the faithful, but sustains it (CCC 880-887).

Catechist's Prayer

Lord Jesus Christ,
you who called the Apostles
and built your Church upon Peter,
give me a heart that loves her as you love her.
May her wounds not scandalize me,
but may her holiness spur me on.
Give me wisdom to teach,
patience to listen,
and charity to welcome each person
as a member of the same Body.
Amen.
