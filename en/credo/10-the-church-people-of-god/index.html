The Church, People of God

Big Question

What is the Church? A building, an institution, or something more?

Saul of Tarsus was on the road to Damascus, «still breathing threats and murder against the disciples of the Lord» (Acts 9:1). He carried letters from the high priest authorizing him to chain any follower of Jesus he found in the city's synagogues. Saul was a man of firm convictions: a Pharisee trained at the feet of Gamaliel, zealous for the Law to the point of fanaticism, convinced that those who preached a crucified man were blaspheming against God.

But when the midday sun erupted in a light brighter than the sun, when he fell to the ground and heard a voice saying «Saul, Saul, why are you persecuting me?», his entire world collapsed. He was not persecuting a sect. He was persecuting a Person. And that Person identified himself with those he was chaining. «I am Jesus, whom you are persecuting» (Acts 9:5).

Saul was left blind. His companions had to lead him by the hand into Damascus. There, a disciple named Ananias —whom God had prepared in a vision— laid hands on him, and something like scales fell from his eyes. Saul regained his sight, rose, and was baptized. The persecutor had become a member of the Body he was persecuting. Thus was born Paul, apostle of Christ, who would later write: «You are the Body of Christ, and each one is a member» (1 Cor 12:27). The Church is not a human organization that Paul decided to join after his conversion. The Church is the very Body of Christ, and Paul discovered that in persecuting it, he was persecuting Christ. What is the Church to you?

Activity

Draw a large tree. In the roots write «Abraham,» on the trunk «Moses» and «David,» on the branches «The Apostles» and «The first Christians,» and on the leaves write your name. Do you see how the Church started long before you were born? You are part of that same story.

The Catechism uses various images to speak to us about the Church (CCC 753-757): it is the sheepfold where Christ is the shepherd, the vineyard where he is the vine and we are the branches, the house of God built with living stones, the bride of Christ, and above all, the Body of Christ and the Temple of the Holy Spirit. No single image suffices on its own; together they help us understand the richness of the mystery of the Church.

The Church, People of God

The Second Vatican Council recovered a central image from Scripture: the Church as «People of God» (CCC 781-782). One does not enter this people by birth, race, or merit, but through faith and Baptism. All the baptized belong to this people with equal dignity. Peter expresses it beautifully: «You are a chosen race, a royal priesthood, a holy nation, a people acquired by God to proclaim the mighty works of him who called you out of darkness into his wonderful light» (1 Pet 2:9).

The People of God has unique characteristics: it is a priestly people (all the baptized share in Christ's priesthood, offering their lives as spiritual worship), a prophetic people (called to proclaim the Gospel through their lives and words), and a royal people (called to reign by serving others, as Christ came to serve, not to be served) (CCC 783-786).

The People of God is not an elite or an exclusive club. It is a universal family, open to all peoples and cultures, because God desires all people to be saved (CCC 781).

Connection

Read 1 Pet 2:9-10. What does Peter say about the identity of Christians? What does it mean that we are a «royal priesthood»? How can you live out your baptismal priesthood in your home, your school, or your workplace?

The Church, Body of Christ and Temple of the Spirit

Paul uses a powerful image: the Church is the Body of Christ (CCC 787-789). Christ is the head and we are the members. But it is not an empty metaphor; it is real. Through Baptism, we are incorporated into Christ, and through the Eucharist, we are united to him intimately. «Because there is one bread, we who are many are one body, for we all partake of the one bread» (1 Cor 10:17).

In this body there is a diversity of members with different gifts and functions, but all are necessary. «If the whole body were an eye, where would the hearing be?» (1 Cor 12:17). No member is more important than another; what matters is unity in Christ (CCC 790-795).

Reflection

The image of the Body of Christ tells us that we cannot be Christians alone. Have you tried to live your faith in isolation? What do you miss when you don't have community? What gifts can you bring to the body?

Furthermore, the Church is the Temple of the Holy Spirit (CCC 797-798). The Holy Spirit is the soul of the Church, the one who gives it life, who guides it, who distributes its gifts. On the day of Pentecost, the Spirit descended upon the Apostles and the Church was born for mission. It is the Spirit who makes the Church the universal sacrament of salvation, the sign and instrument of communion with God and of the unity of the entire human race (CCC 775-776, 799-801).

One, Holy, Catholic, and Apostolic

The Creed confesses that the Church is «one, holy, catholic, and apostolic.» These are not qualities the Church has given itself, but gifts it receives from Christ and that define it (CCC 811-812).

One: The Church is one because it is the Body of one Christ. It has one Lord, one faith, one Baptism (Eph 4:4-6). Although there is a painful division among Christians, the Catholic Church believes that full unity subsists in her, and she works tirelessly for the unity of all Christians through ecumenism (CCC 813-822).

Holy: The Church is holy because its founder is Christ, the Holy One of God, and the Holy Spirit dwells within it. Not because its members are perfect — the Church is made up of sinners — but because Christ continually sanctifies it and makes it a source of holiness through the sacraments. It is at once «chaste and sinful»: holy in its Head, in need of purification in its members (CCC 823-829).

Catholic: Catholic means «universal.» The Church is not for one group, one culture, or one era; it is for everyone. Christ sent his Apostles «to the whole world» (Mk 16:15). The Church is present throughout the world, and in each local community, the whole Church is present (CCC 830-835). Every diocese, every parish, is the Catholic Church in that place.

Apostolic: The Church was built on the foundation of the Apostles (Eph 2:20). Christ chose twelve Apostles, gave them his authority, and sent them to preach the Gospel. That mission continues today through apostolic succession: the bishops, in communion with the Pope — the successor of Peter — are the legitimate successors of the Apostles. The Church preserves the teaching of the Apostles and transmits it faithfully (CCC 857-865).

Going Deeper

The four marks of the Church (one, holy, catholic, and apostolic) are like the coordinates of its identity. Read Eph 4:4-6 and Mt 16:18-19. How does Christ's promise to Peter («You are Peter, and on this rock I will build my Church») relate to the unity and apostolicity of the Church?

Biblical Connection: «You are a chosen race»

You are a chosen race, a royal priesthood, a holy nation, a people acquired by God to proclaim the mighty works of him who called you out of darkness into his wonderful light. Once you were no people, but now you are God's people; once you had not received mercy, but now you have received mercy. 1 Peter 2:9-10

This passage is the «birth certificate» of the People of God in the New Testament. Peter takes the words that the Exodus applied to Israel (Ex 19:5-6) and applies them to the Church: what God did for ancient Israel is now fulfilled in Christ for all peoples. The Church is not an accident of history or a human invention; it is the realization of God's plan, who from the beginning wanted to gather all his children into one family. We are «God's people» because he has chosen us, called us, and given us his mercy. Not because of our merits, but because of his gratuitous love (CCC 781, 803).

For Reflection

  1. When you think of the Church, what is the first thing that comes to mind: an institution, a building, a community? Where does that image come from?
  2. Paul says the Church is the Body of Christ and that we are all members of one another. Have you experienced faith in community, or do you tend to live it alone? What does the Christian community give you?
  3. The Church is holy but is made up of sinners. Do you find it hard to see the Church as something holy when you see the errors and sins of its members, even its pastors? How do you reconcile those two realities?
  4. The Church is catholic, that is, universal. How can you open your heart to Christians from other cultures, other countries, other ways of living the same faith? What can you learn from them?
  5. Apostolic succession ensures that the teaching of the Apostles reaches us today. Do you value the guidance of the Pope and the bishops in your life of faith? Or do you see them as something distant and irrelevant?

Key Takeaway

Key Takeaway: The Church is not a building you go to; you are it, together with all the baptized, the living Body of Christ in the world.

Cultural Resources

«The Calling of Saint Matthew» by Caravaggio — This Baroque masterpiece, in the Contarelli Chapel in Rome, captures the moment Christ calls Matthew, a tax collector, to follow him. Light enters from the right, illuminating Matthew's surprised face, while Christ extends his hand as God extended his to Adam in Creation. It is an image of the Church: an assembly of the called, of sinners who have heard Christ's voice and decided to follow him. Caravaggio, who lived a turbulent life, knew well what it meant to be called from the shadows.

Film «The Great Beauty» (2013) — Although not explicitly a religious film, it has a powerful scene about the Church. The saint (Mother Teresa) visits the writer Jep Gambardella. He asks her: «Why is the Church so sad?» She replies: «The Church is not sad; men are sad. The Church is joyful, because it has the truth.» A reminder that joy is an essential mark of the People of God.

Music: Gregorian chant «Veni Creator Spiritus» — This 9th-century hymn is sung at Pentecost, when the Church celebrates the gift of the Holy Spirit that sends it forth on mission. The Gregorian tradition connects us with centuries of faith lived in community. Listening to it is to join the prayer of millions of Christians throughout history. You can also look for the more contemporary version by Hakuna: «Ven Espíritu Santo.»
