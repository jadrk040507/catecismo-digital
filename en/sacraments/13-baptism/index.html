Baptism

Big Question

What does it mean to be ‘born again’?

A Pharisee named Nicodemus, a ruler of the Jews, came to Jesus at night. He was a learned man, a teacher of Israel, but something about Jesus drew him. And Jesus said to him something that turned his world upside down: «Truly, truly, I say to you, unless one is born again, he cannot see the kingdom of God» (Jn 3:3). Nicodemus was confused: «How can a man be born when he is old? Can he enter a second time into his mother’s womb?» Jesus answered: «Unless one is born of water and the Spirit, he cannot enter the kingdom of God» (Jn 3:5).

That night, Jesus revealed the mystery of Baptism. To be «born again» is not a metaphor. It is a real, sacramental rebirth through water and the Holy Spirit. It is the door of the Church, the first sacrament of the New Law, the beginning of the Christian life. Without Baptism there is no Christianity, because without it there is no new life in Christ.

Baptism in Scripture: The Waters of Salvation

Baptism did not appear suddenly with Jesus. Throughout the Old Testament, God prepared his people through events involving water that prefigured Christian Baptism. The Catechism calls these «figures» or «types» of Baptism (CCC 1217-1222).

The first great figure is the Flood. When God saw that the wickedness of man was great, he sent the waters of the flood to cleanse the earth. But Noah and his family were saved through water, carried in the ark. As St. Peter explains, this water «prefigured Baptism, which now saves you» (1 Pet 3:20-21). The flood destroyed sin but saved the righteous. In Baptism, water destroys sin and saves us in the ark of the Church (CCC 1219).

The second great figure is the Crossing of the Red Sea. Israel was enslaved in Egypt, and God led them out through the waters of the Red Sea. The water that drowned Pharaoh and his army became the salvation of God’s people. St. Paul tells us that our ancestors «were all baptized into Moses in the cloud and in the sea» (1 Cor 10:2). The Red Sea is a figure of Baptism, which frees us from the slavery of sin and leads us into the promised land of the Church (CCC 1221).

Activity

Read the story of the flood (Gen 6-9) and the crossing of the Red Sea (Ex 14). Imagine you are Noah in the ark, or an Israelite crossing the sea on dry ground. What would it feel like to be saved through water? How does that image help you understand your own Baptism?

Other figures include the water from the rock that quenched Israel’s thirst (Ex 17:1-7), which St. Paul identifies with Christ (1 Cor 10:4), and the cleansing of Naaman the Syrian in the Jordan (2 Ki 5:1-14), a clear prefiguration of the healing power of sacramental water (CCC 1222).

Christ Sanctifies the Waters

The Old Testament figures pointed forward to the reality that came in Christ. Jesus himself, though without sin, went to the Jordan River to be baptized by John the Baptist. This was not a baptism of repentance for him, but an act of humility and identification with sinners. And at that moment, the heavens opened, the Holy Spirit descended like a dove, and the voice of the Father was heard: «This is my beloved Son, with whom I am well pleased» (Mt 3:17). By entering the water, Christ sanctified all waters for Baptism (CCC 1223-1225).

The Church Fathers saw in Christ’s baptism the institution of Christian Baptism: «The Lord was baptized, not to be cleansed himself, but to cleanse the waters, so that, being purified by the flesh of Christ that knew no sin, they might have the power to wash away sin» (St. Ambrose). From that moment, water is no longer just water; it is the vehicle of the Holy Spirit, the womb of new life.

Connection

Read the account of Jesus’ baptism in the three Synoptic Gospels (Mt 3:13-17; Mk 1:9-11; Lk 3:21-22). Notice that all three mention the heavens opening, the Spirit descending, and the Father’s voice. The Holy Trinity is revealed at the Jordan. How does this moment connect Baptism to the Trinity?

After his resurrection, Jesus gave the Great Commission: «Go therefore and make disciples of all nations, baptizing them in the name of the Father and of the Son and of the Holy Spirit» (Mt 28:19). Baptism is not an optional extra; it is the command of the risen Lord, the way he established for making disciples. From his pierced side, blood and water flowed (Jn 19:34), which the Fathers saw as the source of the sacraments: from the side of Christ, the new Eve, the Church, was born in blood and water (CCC 1225-1226).

The Sacrament of Baptism

The Catechism teaches that Baptism is the «door of the sacraments,» necessary for salvation for those who have received the Gospel (CCC 1213). Every sacrament involves matter (the visible element), form (the words), a minister (who celebrates), and effects (what it produces). In Baptism these are clearly defined.

Matter and Form

The matter of Baptism is water, real natural water, either by immersion (the most ancient and expressive form) or by pouring (infusion). The water signifies purification, death to sin, and new life (CCC 1239-1240). The form is the Trinitarian formula: «N., I baptize you in the name of the Father, and of the Son, and of the Holy Spirit.» Without these words, there is no Baptism (CCC 1240).

Minister of Baptism

The ordinary minister of Baptism is a bishop, priest, or deacon. However, in case of necessity, any person, even a non-Christian, can baptize, provided they intend to do what the Church does and use the Trinitarian formula (CCC 1256). This is why Baptism is called the «universal sacrament» — no one is ever far from the possibility of being baptized.

Going Deeper

The Catechism explains that the water can be used in three ways: immersion (the baptized person is submerged three times), infusion (water is poured over the head), or aspersion (sprinkling). Immersion best signifies the burial and resurrection with Christ described in Rom 6:3-4. If your parish offers immersion, consider asking about it for your own children or for adult catechumens.

Effects of Baptism

Baptism produces three principal effects (CCC 1262-1274):

  1. Forgiveness of sins: All sins, both original and personal, are forgiven. The baptized person is purified and made a new creation (2 Cor 5:17).
  2. Incorporation into Christ and the Church: The baptized person becomes a member of the Body of Christ, participating in the priesthood of Christ and becoming a temple of the Holy Spirit.
  3. Seal of the Holy Spirit: Baptism imprints an indelible spiritual mark (character) on the soul. This mark never fades and means Baptism can never be repeated (CCC 1272-1274).

Baptism and Original Sin

Why does the Church baptize infants? Why is Baptism necessary? The answer lies in the reality of original sin. We are not born neutral. We are born with a wounded nature, deprived of sanctifying grace, inclined to evil, and separated from God. This state is called original sin, and it is transmitted with human nature, not by imitation but by propagation (CCC 402-406).

Baptism washes away original sin and restores sanctifying grace to the soul. St. Paul writes: «Do you not know that all of us who have been baptized into Christ Jesus were baptized into his death? We were buried therefore with him by baptism into death, so that as Christ was raised from the dead by the glory of the Father, we too might walk in newness of life» (Rom 6:3-4). The baptized person dies to sin and rises to new life in Christ (CCC 1263-1266).

Context

Baptism removes original sin and personal sins, but it does not remove all the consequences of original sin: the tendency to sin (concupiscence), suffering, and death remain. The baptized person still struggles against temptation. But concupiscence is no longer a sin; it is a weakness that grace can overcome. Baptism gives us the grace to fight and to win.

Baptism also makes us children of God: «See what love the Father has given us, that we should be called children of God; and so we are» (1 Jn 3:1). We are adopted into the family of God, brothers and sisters of Christ, heirs of heaven. This is the dignity of every baptized person: a dignity that can never be lost, even if one later sins (CCC 1267-1270).

For Reflection

  1. What did you know about your own Baptism? Do you know where and when you were baptized? Who were your godparents? If you don’t know, ask your parents.
  2. The Big Question asks: «What does it mean to be born again?» How would you answer that question now, after this lesson?
  3. Baptism makes you a child of God, a member of the Church, and a temple of the Holy Spirit. Do you live as if this is true? What would change if you took this dignity seriously?
  4. The water of Baptism destroys sin and gives new life. Is there a sin or habit you need to «drown» in the waters of God’s mercy?
  5. How can you live out your baptismal promises today: to renounce sin and to follow Christ?

Key Takeaway

Key Takeaway: Baptism is the gateway to the Christian life: through water and the Holy Spirit we are born again, freed from sin, incorporated into Christ and his Church, and made children of God.

Memory Verse: «Go therefore and make disciples of all nations, baptizing them in the name of the Father and of the Son and of the Holy Spirit, teaching them to observe all that I have commanded you.» — Mt 28:19-20
