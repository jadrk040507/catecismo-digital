The Seven Petitions

Big Question

What are we really asking for in each petition?

Saint John Chrysostom, the golden-mouthed preacher of fourth-century Antioch, called the Lord's Prayer "the prayer that contains all perfection." He wrote: "When you pray, do not ask for what you already have — you already have a Father in heaven." The seven petitions of the Our Father, Chrysostom taught, are not a shopping list but a transformation of desire. The first three — hallowed be Thy name, Thy kingdom come, Thy will be done — reorient our hearts toward God. The last four — give us this day our daily bread, forgive us our trespasses, lead us not into temptation, deliver us from evil — express our deepest human needs. Seven petitions that contain the whole Gospel. Seven words that change everything.

Understanding Prayer

Prayer is the raising of the mind and heart to God. It is a gift of grace and a determined response on our part. The Catechism teaches that prayer is both contemplation and communion with God.

Activity

When do you most naturally turn to God in prayer? What draws you to prayer?

Biblical Foundation

The Bible is filled with prayers: psalms of praise, laments of suffering, cries for help, and songs of thanksgiving. Jesus Himself taught us to pray.

Connection

Read the Scripture passages and notice how the biblical authors approached God in prayer.

Key Idea

Key Idea: Each petition of the Our Father expresses our fundamental needs and deepest longings.

Cultural Richness

Prayer Tradition: The Church has developed rich traditions of prayer: liturgical, communal, personal, contemplative.

Saints of Prayer: Throughout history, holy men and women have taught us the way of prayer through their example and writings.
