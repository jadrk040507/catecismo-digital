Catechist's Guide — The Dignity of the Human Person

Catechist's Guide

For class preparation and delivery. For the catechist's use only.

Class Summary

Estimated duration: 60 minutes

Central theme: The human person, created in the image of God, possesses an inviolable dignity that grounds all Christian morality.

Objectives:

  • Understand that human dignity comes from being created in the image of God (Gn 1:26-27).
  • Recognize that original sin wounded dignity but did not destroy it.
  • Discover that Christ's redemption restores and elevates our dignity.
  • Learn to live according to one's own dignity and respect that of others.

Key verse: «God created man in his own image; in the image of God he created him; male and female he created them» (Gn 1:27)

CCC references: 1700-1715

Suggested Class Structure

Section Duration What the catechist does
1. Big Question 8 min The story of the child and the coin. Ask: «Where does your dignity come from?»
2. Created in God's image 12 min Explain Gn 1:26-27. The image of God as the foundation of dignity.
3. Dignity of the person 10 min Dimensions of dignity: freedom, conscience, spiritual soul, call to beatitude.
4. Wounds and redemption 10 min Original sin and restoration in Christ. Rom 5:20. «Where sin increased, grace abounded all the more.»
5. For Reflection 10 min Share in pairs the reflection questions. Then discuss in group.
6. Closing: Key Takeaway + Prayer 5 min Read the Key Takeaway. Pray the closing prayer together.
7. Week assignment 5 min Hand out the workbook. Invite them to do the mirror activity every day.

Notes for the Catechist

The image of God

It is important to explain that «image of God» does not mean God has a human body. It means we share divine attributes: intelligence, free will, capacity to love. Use the analogy of a seal that leaves its mark on wax: the mark is not the seal, but it bears its shape.

Dignity vs. self-esteem

Dignity does not depend on how we feel. A person can have low self-esteem but retain all their dignity. Help distinguish between objective value (what we are before God) and subjective value (what we feel). The Gospel invites us to know our objective dignity in order to heal our self-esteem.

Original sin

Be careful when discussing original sin: it is not personal guilt, but an inherited condition that inclines us to evil. It does not mean we are bad by nature; it means we are wounded. Christ has redeemed and restored us. The tone should be hopeful, not condemnatory.

Dignity of all

This lesson has profound social implications: if every person is the image of God, then abortion, euthanasia, discrimination, extreme poverty, and all forms of violence against human beings are offenses against God. Invite participants to connect faith with social realities.

Sensitivity with wounds

Some participants may have a very negative self-image. The phrase «you are the image of God» can be revolutionary for them. Be especially kind and patient. Do not force anyone to share if they do not want to.

Age Adaptation

🌱 Seed (ages 7-12)

Use the story of the crumpled coin. Activity: give each child a small mirror and tell them: «God sees you and loves you.» Draw what it means to be the image of God. Memorize Gn 1:27 with gestures.

🌿 Sprouts (ages 13-17)

Connect with their struggle for identity and acceptance. Social media, peer pressure, and beauty standards challenge their dignity. Ask: «Where do you get your sense of value: from likes or from God?»

🪴 Root (adults new to the faith)

Many adults need to heal their self-image. Help them distinguish between the world's message («you are worth what you do») and God's message («you are worth what you are»). They can share experiences of how faith has transformed their self-perception.

🌳 Tree (formed adults)

Go deeper into the theology of «divinization» in the Church Fathers. Discuss the implications of human dignity for Catholic social teaching: the common good, universal destination of goods, preferential option for the poor.

Difficult Questions

«Isn't evolution incompatible with man being created in the image of God?»

Not necessarily. The Church does not oppose evolution as an explanation for the origin of the human body, but affirms that the spiritual soul is created directly by God (CCC 1705). In any evolutionary process, there is a moment when God infuses a human soul, and that moment marks the creation of a unique person made in his image.

«What about people who do not believe in God? Do they still have dignity?»

Absolutely. Human dignity does not depend on faith in God. All people, whether believers or not, are created in the image of God and possess the same dignity. The Church defends the human rights of all, regardless of religion. «Respect for the human person entails respect for the rights that flow from his dignity as a creature» (CCC 1930).

«How can a person who has gravely sinned retain their dignity?»

Sin does not destroy the image of God, it darkens it. Even the worst sinner retains their fundamental dignity. That is why the Church calls the sinner to conversion: not to recover their dignity (which they never lost), but to live in accordance with it. God never stops loving anyone, no matter how far they have gone.

«What implications does human dignity have for the unborn?»

From conception, the human being is a person and possesses all the dignity of a being created in the image of God. The Catechism is clear: «Human life must be respected and protected absolutely from the moment of conception» (CCC 2270). This is not only Catholic doctrine but a truth of reason: there is no moment in pregnancy when the human being «becomes» a person; they are one from the beginning.

Catechist's Prayer

Father, you who created me in your image,
thank you for the gift of life and dignity.
Help me to see your face in every person I meet,
especially those the world considers insignificant.
Give me words to convey the truth of our dignity,
and a heart that knows how to welcome the wounded.
May my life be a reflection of your creative and redeeming love.
Amen.
