The Dignity of the Human Person

Big Question

What makes you feel valuable? Where does your dignity come from?

A child asked his father: «Dad, how much am I worth?» The father, instead of giving him an easy answer, took a coin and an old crumpled bill. He placed them on the table and asked: «How much is this worth?» «One peso and one hundred pesos,» the child replied. Then the father crumpled the coin, stepped on it, soiled it. «And now?» «It's still worth one peso,» said the child. The father took the bill, tore it in half, and taped it back together. «And now?» «One hundred pesos.» The father smiled: «That's how you are. No matter how much they crumple you, stain you, or break you, your value does not change. Because your value is not set by the world: it is set by God, who created you in his image and likeness.» This is the deepest truth about you: your dignity does not depend on what you do, what you have, or what others think of you. It depends on who created you and what you were made for.

Created in the Image of God

The book of Genesis proclaims a fundamental truth: «God created man in his own image; in the image of God he created him; male and female he created them» (Gn 1:27). This is the source of all human dignity. We are not an accident, not a meaningless evolutionary byproduct. We are created, wanted, loved by God before we were born. And we carry within us something of him: his image (CCC 1701-1702).

Activity

Look at your hands. Think about what they have done today. Now remember: those hands were created by God. You are not a product of chance. You are a masterpiece of God. What changes in your day if you start with this certainty?

Being made in the image of God means we have intelligence to know truth, will to choose good, and the capacity to love. We are persons, not things. We have a value that cannot be quantified, that cannot be bought or sold. «What is man that you are mindful of him?» the Psalmist asks (Ps 8:5). The answer is astonishing: God is mindful of us, he has made us «a little lower than the angels» and crowned us with glory and honor (Ps 8:6-7) (CCC 1703-1704).

Connection

We live in a world that measures people's value by their productivity, appearance, or success. The Christian faith proclaims the opposite: your value is infinite because you are the image of God, regardless of what you can produce or achieve. How does this affect your self-esteem? How does it affect the way you treat others?

The Dignity of the Person

The Catechism teaches that the human person, created in the image of God, possesses a dignity that distinguishes him from all other creatures. This dignity has several dimensions: freedom, moral conscience, the capacity to know God, and the call to beatitude (CCC 1700).

To be a person is not only to have a human body. It is to have a spiritual and immortal soul, created directly by God. As the Catechism says: «The human person, created in the image of God, is a being at once corporeal and spiritual» (CCC 1705). The body is not a prison for the soul nor something shameful; it is an integral part of our identity, also created by God and called to resurrection.

Going Deeper

The Catechism distinguishes between the human being and the rest of creation: only the human being is a «person,» that is, a being endowed with reason and freedom, capable of knowing God and entering into communion with him (CCC 1703). What implications does this have for issues like abortion, euthanasia, or the dignity of the most vulnerable? If every human life is the image of God, then every human life deserves respect, from conception to natural death.

The dignity of the person is also reflected in the fundamental equality of all human beings. «There is neither Jew nor Greek, there is neither slave nor free, there is neither male nor female; for you are all one in Christ Jesus» (Gal 3:28). All discrimination based on race, gender, social condition, or any other characteristic is an offense against human dignity (CCC 1707).

The Wounds of Dignity: Original Sin

But there is a tension in our experience. On one hand, we know we are made for great things, that we have immense dignity. On the other hand, we experience weakness, inclination to evil, interior division. Paul expresses it with pain: «I do not do the good I want, but the evil I do not want is what I do» (Rom 7:19). This is the consequence of original sin: dignity is not lost, but it is wounded (CCC 1706-1707).

Original sin did not destroy the image of God in us, but it darkened it. It left us inclined to evil (concupiscence), vulnerable to suffering and death, and with weakened intellect and will. However, Christ's redemption has restored us. «Where sin increased, grace abounded all the more» (Rom 5:20). In Christ, our dignity is not only restored but elevated: we are children of God (CCC 1708).

Going Deeper

The Church Fathers spoke of the «divinization» of the Christian: God became man so that man might become God (St. Athanasius, St. Irenaeus). This is not an exaggeration: by grace, we participate in the divine nature (2 Pt 1:4). This means that the Christian's dignity is even greater than that of the first Adam: not only the image of God, but a son in the Son. How does this change your understanding of yourself?

Living According to Your Dignity

The Catechism invites us to live in accordance with our dignity. «Christian, recognize your dignity,» said St. Leo the Great. «Now that you have become a partaker of the divine nature, do not return to your former base condition.» Knowing our dignity demands that we live consistently: we cannot treat ourselves or others as objects (CCC 1709).

Living according to one's dignity means:

  • Respecting one's own body as a temple of the Holy Spirit (1 Cor 6:19)
  • Seeking the truth and adhering to it
  • Using freedom to choose the good
  • Treating others with the respect they deserve as persons
  • Defending the life and dignity of the most vulnerable

Ultimately, living according to our dignity means living as children of God, destined for eternal beatitude. We are not made for a mediocre life; we are made for holiness, for full happiness in God (CCC 1710-1715).

Biblical Connection: «Let us make man in our image»

Scripture begins with an act of creative love: «Let us make man in our image, after our likeness» (Gn 1:26). Note that God says «let us make» in the plural, already hinting at Trinitarian communion. The human being is not created in solitude but in relationship, as the image of a God who is a communion of love.

This truth is so important that the Catechism places it as the foundation of all Christian morality: «The human person, created in the image of God, is called to beatitude» (CCC 1700). All Christian morality is understood from here: it is not a set of arbitrary rules, but the path to living in accordance with our true identity, the path to being who we are called to be.

For Reflection

  1. Where does your sense of personal value really come from? From your work, your relationships, your appearance, or from knowing that you are the image of God?
  2. What people in your life do you find it hard to recognize as «the image of God»? What would change if you looked at them from this truth?
  3. Original sin wounded our dignity but did not destroy it. In what areas of your life do you feel this «wound» most strongly?
  4. «Christian, recognize your dignity» (St. Leo the Great). What does this mean for how you live your daily life?
  5. If you truly believed you were a child of God, what would you change in your thinking, speaking, and acting?

Key Takeaway

Key Takeaway: Your dignity is not something you can earn or lose; it is something you already have because God created you in his image. Live accordingly.

Cultural Resources

«The Creation of Adam» by Michelangelo — In the Sistine Chapel, Michelangelo painted God extending his hand toward Adam to give him life. The fingers are about to touch. That image captures the moment when God communicates his image to man. Man is not an evolved animal; he is a creature touched by God.

St. John Paul II, «Theology of the Body» — The Polish Pope dedicated a full cycle of catechesis to the dignity of the human body. He taught that the body «manifests» the person and that human love is a reflection of God's love. His phrase «Man cannot live without love» summarizes the vocation of the person: we are made to love and be loved.

Mother Teresa of Calcutta — She saw Christ in every person, especially the poorest and most abandoned. She said, «Each person is unique and created by God with a purpose.» Her life was a testimony that recognizing the dignity of every human being transforms the world.
