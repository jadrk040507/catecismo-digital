God is Father

Big Question

How do you imagine God receives you when you return to Him?

He saw him from afar. Even when the son was nothing but a dusty figure on the horizon, the father recognized him. He did not wait sitting down. He did not cross his arms. He did not say, «Let him come and beg for forgiveness on his knees.» He ran. An elderly man running down the road, his tunic lifted, caring nothing about what others would say, caring nothing about shame. And when he reached him, he did not ask for explanations, he did not demand a speech of repentance: he embraced him. He embraced him and covered him with kisses. That is the image Jesus gives us of God. Not a judge waiting to punish, but a Father who runs toward us before we finish turning back. Can you imagine that God is like this? Can you believe that God loves you in that way, without conditions, without reproach, without waiting for you to earn it?

God's Name: «I Am»

At the burning bush, Moses asks God a question that lies in the heart of all humanity: «What is your name?» (Ex 3:13). He wants to know who this God is who calls him. And God answers with something mysterious and profound: «I am who I am» — YHWH (Ex 3:14). This name is not just a label; it is a presence. God does not say «my name is such-and-such»; he says «I am here, I am.» He is the God who exists by himself, who depends on nothing, who is the source of everything that is. And that God, Being itself, is the one who reveals himself as Father (CCC 205-206).

Activity

Write your full name and, next to each letter, a word that describes something important about you. Now imagine: God introduces himself as «I Am.» What does that tell you about him? What does it mean that God needs no other name than «I Am»?

The name YHWH was so holy for the people of Israel that it was never pronounced. Instead, they said «Lord» (Adonai). This reverence reminds us that God is infinite mystery, but a mystery that draws near, that reveals himself, that wants to be known (CCC 206-207).

God the Father in the Old Testament

The revelation of God as Father does not begin in the New Testament. Already in the Old Testament, God presents himself as father of his people. Moses reminds Israel: «Is he not your father, who created you?» (Dt 32:6). The prophet Isaiah cries out: «You, Lord, are our father» (Is 63:16). And Psalm 103 describes God's heart with stunning tenderness: «As a father has compassion on his children, so the Lord has compassion on those who fear him» (Ps 103:13) (CCC 238).

Connection

Look up Dt 32:6; Is 63:16; and Ps 103:13. Underline the word «father» in each passage. What image of God do these texts give you? In what ways does this father resemble a human father? In what ways is he different?

Israel experienced God as a father throughout centuries: in the liberation from Egypt, in the covenant at Sinai, in the exile and return. Little by little, they discovered that God is not a distant, cold being, but someone who loves with a father's heart: who corrects when necessary, who forgives without measure, who never abandons (CCC 239).

Jesus Reveals the Father: «Abba»

What the Old Testament hinted at, Jesus reveals fully. The radical novelty of Jesus is not that God is Father — that was already known — but how he is Father. Jesus uses a word that no one dared to use when addressing God: Abba. It is the word a small child uses to speak to his daddy. Not «Almighty Lord,» not «Creator of the universe.» It is «Daddy.» «Abba, Father,» Jesus says in Gethsemane (Mk 14:36). And he teaches us to pray like this: «Our Father» (Mt 6:9) (CCC 240-241).

Reflection

The word «Abba» was scandalous in Jesus' time. No pious Jew would have addressed God with such familiarity. What does that tell us about the relationship Jesus has with the Father? What does it tell us about the relationship we are invited into?

Jesus does not merely call God Father; he teaches us that God is Father in his deepest being. «No one knows the Father except the Son and anyone to whom the Son chooses to reveal him» (Mt 11:27). Jesus is the definitive revelation of the Father: whoever sees him, sees the Father (Jn 14:9) (CCC 240).

God is Love

John says it with a clarity that leaves no room for doubt: «God is love» (1 Jn 4:8,16). He does not say that God has love, but that God is love. Love is not something God does; it is what God is. His essence, his deepest identity, is love. For this reason he can be Father: because love, by its nature, gives itself, pours itself out, generates life. The Father loves the Son from all eternity, and from that love proceeds the Holy Spirit. God is a communion of love, a divine family (CCC 221-222).

Going Deeper

If «God is love» (1 Jn 4:8), then everything God does is an act of love: creation, covenant, incarnation, cross, resurrection. How does your understanding of God change if you begin with «God is Love» instead of «God is Power»? What implications does this have for your life of faith?

The Parable of the Prodigal Son: The Father Who Waits

Jesus told this story in response to those who criticized him for welcoming sinners (Lk 15:1-2). The parable has three characters: the younger son who leaves, the older son who stays, and the father who loves both. But the true protagonist is the father. He is the one who divides the inheritance without reproach, who waits every day looking at the horizon, who runs to meet the returning son, who throws the feast. And he is also the one who goes out to the field to find the older son, bitter and resentful, to invite him into the joy (Lk 15:11-32) (CCC 239).

Activity

Read Lk 15:20-24 aloud. Count how many actions of the father you can find (running, embracing, kissing, giving the ring, giving the sandal, killing the fattened calf, throwing a party...). What does the sheer number of the father's actions tell you about what God is like?

The father in the parable is neither permissive nor absent. He is a father who respects the freedom of his children — even when they make bad choices — and who never stops waiting, never stops loving, never closes the door. That is the definitive image Jesus gives us of God: a Father who does not treat us as slaves, but as sons and daughters (CCC 240).

Children, Not Slaves: Filial Trust

Paul states it powerfully: «You did not receive a spirit of slavery to fall back into fear, but you received a spirit of adoption, through which we cry, 'Abba, Father!'» (Rom 8:15). The Christian faith is not the religion of fear. We are not before a God who watches us to punish us. We are before a Father who has made us his children by grace. This changes the root of our relationship with him: it is not obedience out of fear, but a response of love (CCC 241, 2782).

Filial trust does not mean that everything will be easy or that there will be no suffering. It means that, in the midst of everything, we know who holds us. A child trusts his father not because everything goes well, but because he knows he is loved. That is the trust Jesus asks of us: «Do not be afraid, little flock, for it is your Father's good pleasure to give you the kingdom» (Lk 12:32) (CCC 2734).

Connection

Think of a moment when you felt that God was far away or not listening to you. How does that experience change if you look at it from the trust that you are a child and not a slave? Write a short prayer beginning with «Abba, Father...»

God is Truth, Almighty, and Creator

The Creed professes: «I believe in God, the Father almighty, Creator of heaven and earth.» Each word carries deep meaning. Father: because he loves us with a father's heart, not as a distant being. Almighty: not a power that dominates, but a power that creates, saves, rescues. God's omnipotence is not tyranny; it is the force of his love that nothing can stop (CCC 268-271). Creator: because everything that exists comes from his free love. He did not need to create; he created out of love, to share his life and joy (CCC 295-296).

Reflection

If God is all-powerful, why does he allow evil? This is one of the hardest questions of faith. The Catechism answers that God permits evil out of respect for our freedom, but that from every evil he can bring forth a greater good (CCC 311-314). The Cross is the definitive proof: from the worst human evil, God brought the greatest salvation.

God is Truth: God is light and in him there is no darkness (1 Jn 1:5). He cannot deceive or be deceived. His word is faithful, his promises are fulfilled. We can trust him completely because he is Truth itself (CCC 214-217).

Biblical Connection: «I Am Who I Am»

God said to Moses, «I AM WHO I AM.» And he said, «Say this to the people of Israel: 'I AM has sent me to you.'» Exodus 3:14

This name — YHWH — reveals something astonishing: God is not defined in relation to anything else («I am the god of...»), but by himself. He is Being, the source of all existence. And that God who is Being itself is the same God who reveals himself as Father. The «I Am» of the burning bush is the «Abba» of Jesus. The most high and inaccessible God is the close and loving Father. There are not two gods: one of the Old and one of the New Testament. He is the same God, fully revealed in Christ (CCC 205-213).

For Reflection

  1. What strikes you most about the image of the father in the parable of the Prodigal Son: that he ran, that he embraced him, that he threw the feast? Why?
  2. If God is «Father,» how does that change the way you pray? Do you pray more like a slave asking permission or a child trusting?
  3. Jesus uses the word «Abba» — like a child saying «Daddy.» Do you find it difficult to relate to God with that closeness? What holds you back?
  4. «God is Love» (1 Jn 4:8). How do you understand suffering in the world from this truth? What answer does the Cross give you?
  5. When in your life have you experienced God as a Father who waits for you or runs to meet you?

Key Takeaway

Key Takeaway: God is not a judge waiting for you to fail; he is a Father who runs to meet you before you finish turning back.

Cultural Resources

«The Return of the Prodigal Son» by Rembrandt — This painting, housed in the Hermitage Museum in St. Petersburg, is one of the most moving works in art history. Rembrandt painted the embrace between father and son with a warm light that seems to come from heaven itself. What makes it extraordinary is that Rembrandt painted this at the end of his life, when he himself had lost everything: money, fame, family. Perhaps he recognized himself in that returning son. Look at the father's hands: they are different. It is said that one hand is a man's and the other a woman's, symbolizing the maternal and paternal love of God.

Film «The Mission» (1986) — In the final scene, Father Gabriel walks among the soldiers carrying the Blessed Sacrament, singing, while guns fire around him. He does not meet violence with violence. It is the image of a love that does not defend itself because it trusts completely in the Father. It is filial trust taken to its ultimate consequence: «Abba, Father, all things are possible to you; remove this cup from me; yet not what I will, but what you will» (Mk 14:36).

Song «Abba Padre» by Hakuna — This musical movement began in Spain and has spread throughout the Spanish-speaking world (also available in English). The song expresses with simplicity what Jesus revealed: that God is «Abba,» a close Father who loves us without conditions. It is a good way to enter into the filial trust that the Catechism proposes.
