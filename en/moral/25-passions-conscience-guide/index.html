Catechist's Guide — The Passions and Moral Conscience

Catechist's Guide

For preparing and leading the class.

Class Summary

Estimated time: 60 minutes

Core topic: The passions (emotions) are a gift from God that must be ordered by reason enlightened by faith, and conscience is the inner voice that guides us to distinguish good from evil.

Objectives:

  • Understand that passions are not evil in themselves; their morality depends on how we use them.
  • Understand what moral conscience is and why it must be formed.
  • Distinguish between certain, doubtful, and erroneous conscience.
  • Recognize how passions can cloud or help the judgment of conscience.

Key verse: «Blessed are the pure in heart, for they shall see God» (Mt 5:8)

Catechism references: CCC 1762-1802

Suggested Class Structure

Section Time What the Catechist Does
1. Big Question 10 min Carlos’s story. Ask: «Has this happened to you? What would you do differently?»
2. The Passions 12 min Explain passions as a natural gift. Ask for examples. Use the fire metaphor.
3. Conscience 12 min Define conscience as «inner sanctuary.» Explain three states. Discuss formation.
4. Passion & Conscience 10 min How passions cloud or help. Concrete examples.
5. Biblical Connection 6 min Mt 5:8 and Rom 7:19. St. Paul’s inner struggle as universal human experience.
6. Reflection & Close 10 min Reflection questions. Small group sharing if trust allows.

Notes for the Catechist

Key point: The great temptation is to fall into two extremes: (1) demonizing emotions («feelings are bad, suppress them») or (2) divinizing them («follow your feelings»). The Catholic position is ordering: passions are good, but must be integrated under the rule of reason enlightened by faith.

St. Thomas on the passions: He dedicated a whole section of the Summa to the passions (I-II, q.22-48). He distinguishes between concupiscible passions (love, hate, desire, aversion, joy, sadness) and irascible passions (hope, despair, boldness, fear, anger). Good depth for the Tree level.

Ignatian discernment: St. Ignatius developed 14 rules for discerning spirits. The core idea: not all interior movements come from God. Learning to distinguish them is essential for ordering passions according to conscience.

Age Adaptation

🌱 Seed (Ages 7-12)

  • Simplify: emotions are like a horse you must learn to tame. God gave us the reins (conscience).
  • Activity: draw a horse or car with «brakes» that are the virtues.
  • Avoid erroneous conscience. Focus on God helping us know right from wrong.

🌿 Sprout (Ages 13-17)

  • Connect to their experience: social media, peer pressure, relationships.
  • Provocative question: «If something feels right, is it automatically good? If it feels wrong, is it automatically bad?»
  • Discuss the Examen as a self-knowledge tool.

🪴 Root (Adults new in faith)

  • May have wounds from the past: «I was raised with guilt.» Distinguish false guilt (psychological) from real guilt (sin).
  • Emphasis on mercy: conscience is not an implacable judge, but the Father’s voice guiding us.

🌳 Tree (Formed adults)

  • Develop St. Thomas’s distinction between synderesis (habit of first moral principles) and conscience (application to concrete cases).
  • Discuss the problem of erroneous conscience: does it oblige even if wrong?
  • Relationship with the cardinal virtues: prudence guides conscience.

Difficult Questions

1. «So is it a sin to feel anger?»

No. Feeling anger is not a sin. Jesus felt anger in the Temple. The sin is what you do with that anger: insult, attack, hold grudges. Rightly ordered anger can be just: indignation at injustice.

2. «How do I know if my conscience is well-formed?»

A well-formed conscience is in harmony with the teaching of the Church, Scripture, and the natural law. If you have doubts, consult a priest or spiritual director and study the Catechism.

3. «Can I follow my conscience even if it’s wrong?»

You must follow your conscience, but you also have an obligation to form it. If you know your conscience might be wrong and do nothing to correct it, you are responsible. If the error is invincible (you had no way to know better), God does not hold it against you.

Catechist’s Prayer

Lord Jesus, Teacher of the human heart,
You who know our inner struggles,
give us wisdom to guide those entrusted to us.
Help us teach that emotions are not enemies
but gifts that need to be ordered by Your grace.
May our own conscience be well formed
so we can guide others on the path of goodness.
Amen.
