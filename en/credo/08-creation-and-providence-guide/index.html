Catechist's Guide — Creation and Providence

Catechist's Guide

For class preparation and delivery. For the catechist's use only.

Class Summary

Estimated duration: 60 minutes

Central theme: God created the world out of nothing by love and governs it with his providence; evil does not contradict the goodness of God.

Objectives:

  • Understand that God created everything out of nothing, not out of necessity but out of love.
  • Recognize that creation is a Trinitarian work: Father, Son, and Holy Spirit.
  • Internalize that God declared his creation «very good» and that matter is not evil.
  • Understand divine providence and how it relates to freedom and evil.
  • Trust that God sustains creation at every moment and brings good out of evil.

Key verse: «You love all things that exist, and you hate none of the things you have made, for you would not have made anything if you had hated it» (Wis 11:24)

CCC references: 279-324

Suggested Class Structure

Section Duration What the catechist does
1. Big Question (Augustine and the child) 10 min Tell the story of Saint Augustine and the child on the beach. Ask: «If God didn’t need to create anything, why does everything that exists exist?»
2. God, Creator of heaven and earth 10 min Explain the first article of the Creed. Activity: look out the window and say «God wanted this to exist.»
3. Creation, work of the Trinity / Creatio ex nihilo 12 min Trinitarian connection (Jn 1:1-3; Gen 1:2). Then explain creatio ex nihilo: God did not shape eternal matter, he called into existence what did not exist. Emphasize that he created out of love, not necessity (CCC 290-295).
4. Angels and the visible world / Providence and evil 15 min Brief mention of angels and the goodness of the material. Then address the hardest question: why does evil exist? God permits evil but does not will it; from every evil he brings a greater good. The cross is the proof (CCC 302-314).
5. Biblical Connection and For Reflection 8 min Read Jer 10:12-13 aloud. Then invite small group sharing on one of the 5 reflection questions.
6. Closing: Key Takeaway + Prayer 5 min Read the Key Takeaway. Pray the workbook prayer together slowly.

Notes for the Catechist

The story of Saint Augustine

It is a legend, not a historical fact, but it serves as a hook. The point is not the impossibility of understanding God, but that this incomprehensible God chose to create out of love. Make sure the group does not stay at «God is incomprehensible» but moves toward «God is love that creates.»

Creatio ex nihilo

This concept is fundamental. In the ancient world, many religions believed in eternal matter. Biblical revelation is unique: God does not battle chaos, he dominates it with his word. He does not shape eternal clay; he calls into existence what did not exist. It is important for the group to understand that this is not a scientific theory but a theological truth: the universe depends totally on God.

The goodness of creation

Many people, even Christians, have a negative view of the body and matter. It is important to emphasize that God declared his creation «very good» (Gen 1:31). Matter is not evil; it is God’s gift. This opposes both materialism (which reduces everything to matter) and dualism (which despises matter).

The problem of evil

It is almost certain that someone will ask about suffering. Do not offer easy answers. The essential point: God does not will evil, he permits it while respecting freedom, and from every evil he brings a greater good. The cross is the definitive proof. You can cite Saint Thomas: «God permits evil in order to bring from it a greater good.» It is not about justifying suffering, but about trusting that God does not lose control.

Age Adaptation

🌱 Seed (ages 7-12)

Use the image of a painter who paints a picture for pleasure, not because someone paid him. Activities: draw something from creation and write «God wanted this to exist,» color the days of creation, memorize Wis 11:24 with gestures.

🌿 Sprouts (ages 13-17)

Connect with the question of evil: why is there suffering if God is good? Do not dodge the question. Use the film «The Tree of Life» by Malick as a reference. Providence is not a remote control; it is a presence that accompanies. The cross transforms evil, it does not cancel it.

🪴 Root (adults new to the faith)

Many adults have suffered injustice and find it hard to trust in providence. Be sensitive: do not minimize pain. Help them see that faith is not an explanation of evil, but a trust in the midst of evil. God is not absent; he is present even when we do not feel him. The example of Job can help.

🌳 Tree (formed adults)

Go deeper into the doctrine of providence: God causes everything in a primary way, creatures in a secondary way (CCC 308). Human freedom does not compete with providence but cooperates with it. You can cite Saint Augustine and Saint Thomas on the relationship between freedom and providence.

Difficult Questions

«If God is good and all-powerful, why does he permit evil?»

This is the oldest and most difficult question of faith. The Catechism does not offer an easy answer, but it teaches that God respects the freedom of his creatures and that from every evil he can bring a greater good (CCC 309-314). The cross is the definitive proof: from the worst crime in history, God brought the greatest salvation. It is not about understanding evil, but about trusting that God is present even in suffering.

«Does science contradict creation?»

No. Faith and science answer different questions. Science explains how; faith answers why and what for. The Genesis account is not a biology textbook, but a revelation about the origin and meaning of the world (CCC 283-289). Catholics can accept evolution as a scientific tool while affirming that God is the ultimate creator of everything.

«Are angels real or are they symbols?»

They are real. The Catechism teaches that angels are spiritual, personal, and immortal beings, created by God as «servants and messengers» of his plan of salvation (CCC 329-330). They are not pious ornaments or ancient myths. They actively participate in the history of salvation. But we should not fall into exaggerated angelology that distracts from Christ.

«What does it mean that God ‘sustains’ creation at every moment?»

It means that creation is not a past event that God «left running.» Without God’s continuous action, everything would return to nothing. «In him we live and move and have our being» (Acts 17:28). Your existence at this very second depends on God’s creative love, not only on the love that acted at the beginning, but on the love that acts now (CCC 301).

Catechist's Prayer

Lord, you who created the world out of nothing
and sustain it with your love at every moment,
give me the grace to transmit this truth with fire:
that nothing exists by chance,
that everything is a gift of your love,
that evil does not have the last word.
Help me to help others trust in your providence,
even when they do not understand the path.
May my life be a witness that you sustain all things.
Amen.
