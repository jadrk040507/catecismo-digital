Catechist's Guide — God is Father

Catechist's Guide

For class preparation and delivery. For the catechist's use only.

Class Summary

Estimated duration: 60 minutes

Central theme: God reveals himself as Father in Scripture, and Jesus teaches us to relate to him with filial trust.

Objectives:

  • Understand that God's name («I AM») reveals his being and his closeness.
  • Discover that God is Father from the Old Testament onward.
  • Know the radical novelty of Jesus: God is Abba, Daddy.
  • Interiorize that «God is Love» (1 Jn 4:8).
  • Live out filial trust as children, not as slaves.

Key verse: «You did not receive a spirit of slavery to fall back into fear, but you received a spirit of adoption, through which we cry, 'Abba, Father!'» (Rom 8:15)

CCC references: 198-231, 239-241, 268-271, 295-296

Suggested Class Structure

Section Duration What the catechist does
1. Big Question (Prodigal Son) 10 min Read or tell the parable from the father's perspective. Ask: «How do you imagine God receives you when you return to him?»
2. God's Name: I AM 10 min Explain Ex 3:13-15. The name YHWH as presence and closeness. Activity: write names and think about «I AM».
3. God the Father in OT / Abba 12 min Dt 32:6; Is 63:16; Ps 103:13. Then the novelty of Jesus: Abba. Look up the Scripture passages together.
4. God is Love / Children not Slaves 10 min 1 Jn 4:8.16; Rom 8:15. Reflection on filial trust.
5. For Reflection 8 min Ask participants to share one of the 5 reflection questions in small groups, then plenary.
6. Closing: Key Takeaway + Prayer 5 min Read the Key Takeaway. Pray the Our Father slowly together, emphasizing «Father».
7. Week assignment 5 min Hand out the workbook. Invite them to write the letter to God as Father during the week.

Notes for the Catechist

The Prodigal Son parable

The key is telling it from the father's perspective, not the son's. The center is the father's heart. Help the group notice that the father runs (undignified for an older man in that culture), embraces and kisses (gestures of tenderness that break through distance), and throws a feast (total restoration of the son's dignity).

The name YHWH

Explain that for the Jews it was so sacred it was never pronounced. This helps us understand the reverence owed to God. But Jesus invites us to call him «Abba,» a closeness that does not remove reverence but transforms it into filial trust.

«God is Love»

This phrase (1 Jn 4:8) is the heart of Christian revelation. It is not an abstract definition; it is a declaration of identity. God is not primarily power, justice, or wisdom (though he is also these), but love. Everything else is understood from this.

Filial trust vs. slavery

Some participants may have a distorted image of God as a severe judge waiting for them to fail. Help them see that the Gospel presents exactly the opposite. Ask: «Where does this image of a severe God come from?»

The problem of evil

Someone will almost certainly ask about suffering. You don't need a complete answer. Point to the Cross as God's response to evil: not an explanation, but a redeeming presence. God does not eliminate suffering, but assumes it and transforms it.

Age Adaptation

🌱 Seed (ages 7-12)

Use the metaphor of water and thirst: God is like a dad who gives us water when we are thirsty. Activities: draw the burning bush, color the father embracing the son, memorize «God is Love» with hand signs.

🌿 Sprouts (ages 13-17)

Connect with their experience of authority: Have they had a father figure who disappointed them? How does that relate to the image of God? Use examples from movies or shows about fathers. The word «Abba» can help break the image of a distant God.

🪴 Root (adults new to the faith)

Many adults carry wounds from their father figure. Be sensitive: not everyone has a good experience of fatherhood. Help them distinguish between human fatherhood (limited, sometimes failed) and divine fatherhood (perfect, unconditional). Testimony from converts who discovered God as Father can be very powerful.

🌳 Tree (formed adults)

Go deeper into the Trinity: the Father loves the Son from all eternity, and that love is the Holy Spirit. God's fatherhood is not a metaphor; it is a theological reality. You can quote the Church Fathers (St. Augustine, St. John Chrysostom) on divine fatherhood.

Difficult Questions

«If God is an almighty Father, why does he let innocent people suffer?»

This is perhaps the hardest question of faith. The Catechism offers no easy answer, but points out that God respects our freedom and that from every evil he can bring forth a greater good. The Cross is the supreme example: the worst crime in history became the greatest salvation. It is not about understanding evil, but trusting that God is present even in suffering (CCC 311-314).

«Isn't it sexist to call God «Father»? Does God have gender?»

God has no biological gender. The Catechism says God is «neither man nor woman» (CCC 239). However, Jesus revealed God as «Father» and Scripture uses this language. God's fatherhood does not imply gender, but a relationship: he is the origin of all things, the one who gives life, who loves unconditionally. There are also maternal images of God in the Bible (Is 49:15; 66:13), showing that all human fatherhood and motherhood are imperfect reflections of God's love.

«What if I had a bad father and I struggle to call God «Father»?»

This is very understandable. The image of God as Father can be painful for those who have suffered abuse, abandonment, or indifference from their earthly father. In that case, it can help to begin with the image of God as «Abba» (a new word, without negative baggage), or to focus on the maternal aspects of God. The key is to remember that divine fatherhood is the measure of all human fatherhood, not the other way around (Eph 3:14-15). God's love heals and transcends all our human experiences.

«Isn't the God of the Old Testament more of a severe judge than a loving Father?»

It is a common mistake to think the OT shows an angry God and the NT a loving God. In the OT we also find a God full of mercy, who forgives Israel again and again (Hos 11:1-9; Ps 103:8-14). And in the NT there is also judgment (Mt 25:31-46). The Catechism explains that there is a single progressive revelation: the God who creates, chooses, liberates, and forgives in the OT is the same God who reveals himself as Father in Jesus Christ (CCC 238).

Catechist's Prayer

Father, you who are the source of all love,
thank you for calling me to serve you in catechesis.
Give me a father's heart for those who listen to me:
patience when they do not understand,
tenderness when they doubt,
firmness when they stray.
May I not be an obstacle to their knowing you,
but a reflection of your loving fatherhood.
May seeing me help them glimpse who you are.
Amen.
