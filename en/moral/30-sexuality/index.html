Human Sexuality

Big Question

Why did God create us male and female? What is the purpose of our sexuality?

In 1979, John Paul II began a series of audiences that would last five years. Every Wednesday, in St. Peter's Square, the Pope spoke about a subject few expected: the human body, sexuality, marriage. He did not speak about prohibitions or rules. He spoke about mystery. About love. About God. What he called the "Theology of the Body" revolutionized the way we understand human sexuality. It is not something shameful to hide, nor an animal instinct to repress, nor a cultural whim that each person defines for themselves. It is a language, a visible sign of an invisible reality. Our bodies — masculine and feminine — say something about God. They say that God is love. That love is total self-gift. That life springs from love. This is the truth the Church proclaims about sexuality: it is not a problem, it is a gift. It is not a burden, it is a vocation. It is not an accident of evolution, it is a divine plan.

Sexuality: Gift and Vocation

Sexuality is part of the being of man and woman, because it was given and left by God. God created man and woman and blessed them saying: "Be fruitful and multiply" (Gn 1:28). Sexuality is not something we have, but something we are. It constitutes us as persons capable of loving and giving life (CCC 2331-2336).

Being man or woman is not a social construction or an individual choice. It is a reality given by God, grounded in the bodily and spiritual nature of the human being. "Male and female He created them" (Gn 1:27). This sexual difference is fundamental to human identity. It is not a limitation, but a richness. Not a division, but a complementarity (CCC 2333-2336).

Sexuality has two inseparable dimensions: union (to love) and procreation (to give life). These two purposes are ordered by God and cannot be arbitrarily separated. Conjugal love, when lived according to God's plan, is fruitful. Fruitfulness, when it is the fruit of true love, sanctifies (CCC 2337-2347).

Activity

Read Genesis 2:18-25: the creation of woman. Notice that Eve was not created to serve Adam, nor Adam to dominate Eve. They were created for each other, "a suitable helper." What does this say about equality and complementarity between man and woman? How has modern culture seen this relationship, and how does Scripture present it?

Chastity

Chastity is the integration of sexuality into the person. It means the harmonious unification and mastery of the body's impulses by the will, supported by grace. Chastity protects the intimacy of the person and enables them to enter into relationship with others from their own self (CCC 2337-2339).

Chastity is not repression. It is integration. It is not denying sexuality, but ordering it to true love. It is the strength that frees us from the slavery of concupiscence and enables us for authentic love. It is the capacity to say "no" to what does not belong and "yes" to what is true (CCC 2341-2345).

Chastity has different modalities according to state of life: celibacy for the Kingdom, chastity of the single person awaiting marriage, fidelity of the married spouse, continence of the widowed. But for all, chastity is a gift from God, a virtue to be cultivated, a grace to be sought (CCC 2346-2350).

Connection

Current culture confuses chastity with "lack of experience" or "sexual repression." But chastity is strength, not weakness. It is the capacity to say "no" to what does not correspond and "yes" to what is true. Do you know examples of chaste people in your life? What characterizes them? They are not frustrated people; they are free people, capable of truly loving.

The Sixth Commandment: "You Shall Not Commit Adultery"

The Sixth Commandment, according to Jesus' teaching, not only forbids external acts of adultery, but also lustful desires in the heart. "You have heard that it was said: 'You shall not commit adultery.' But I say to you: everyone who looks at a woman with lust has already committed adultery with her in his heart" (Mt 5:27-28) (CCC 2380-2381).

This commandment protects conjugal fidelity, purity of heart, and the dignity of the person. Adultery destroys the matrimonial bond, grievously wounds the spouses, harms the children, and offends against the dignity of marriage. It is a grave injustice because it breaks the promise of fidelity, the basis of conjugal love (CCC 2380-2382).

But Jesus does not limit Himself to prohibiting. He offers a radical transformation of the heart. "If your right eye causes you to sin, tear it out... If your right hand causes you to sin, cut it off" (Mt 5:29-30). These hyperbolic expressions do not call for physical mutilation, but for radicality in fighting temptation. The root of what separates us from God must be cut (CCC 2383-2391).

Deepening

Lust is "the disordered desire for sexual pleasure." It is not the same as legitimate attraction or a healthy desire for conjugal union. Lust reduces the other person to an object of pleasure, ignoring their personal dignity. Chastity, on the other hand, sees the other person as a person, image of God, worthy of love. The fight against lust is not denying sexuality, but purifying it so that it can fulfill its vocation: love given, not pleasure consumed.

Marriage and Family

Marriage is the original vocation of human sexuality. "For this reason a man shall leave his father and mother and be joined to his wife, and they shall become one flesh" (Gn 2:24). Conjugal love is ordered to the good of the spouses and to the procreation and education of children. It is a sacrament that configures the spouses with Christ, who loves His Church (CCC 2392-2396).

The family is the "vital cell of society." It is the first place where one learns to love, to respect, to forgive. Parents are the first educators of their children in faith and morals. The Church has a special mission to protect and promote the family against everything that weakens it (CCC 2397-2400).

Christian conjugal love has characteristic features: it is total (gives everything, not just a part), faithful (until death do them part), fruitful (open to life), exclusive (only between the spouses). These characteristics are not arbitrary impositions, but the way true love manifests itself (CCC 2397-2400).

Expansion

Ephesians 5:25-33 compares marriage to the relationship between Christ and the Church. Husbands must love their wives "as Christ loved the Church and gave himself up for her." This transforms marriage: it is not just a social contract, but a sacrament, a mystery, a sign of God's love. How does the perspective of marriage change if spouses think their love must reflect Christ's love for His Church?

Biblical Connection: The Wedding at Cana

"Now there were six stone water jars... Jesus said to them: 'Fill the jars with water.'... when the manager tasted the water that had become wine... he said: 'You have kept the good wine until now'."

John 2:1-12

The first miracle of Jesus was at a wedding. This was not a coincidence. Jesus blessed marriage with His presence and with His power. The water turned into wine is a symbol of how Christ transforms human love: He makes it more abundant, more joyful, more fruitful. The "good wine" kept until the end is true love, sacrificial love, love that gives its life.

Jesus also taught about the indissolubility of marriage: "For this reason a man shall leave his father and mother and be joined to his wife, and they shall become one flesh. So they are no longer two, but one flesh. What God has joined together, let no man separate" (Mt 19:5-6). This is the truth of Christian marriage: it is a sign of God's fidelity to His people, indissoluble as God's love (CCC 2397-2400).

For Reflection

  1. What does today's culture understand by "sexuality," and what does the Church understand? What are the most important differences?
  2. Chastity is not "lack of sex," but "integration of sexuality." How do you live this integration in your current state of life?
  3. Jesus raises the Sixth Commandment to the level of the heart. What does this mean for the purity of your thoughts, gazes, and desires?
  4. If a friend asks you why the Church opposes sex outside of marriage, what would you say? How would you explain that this is protection, not prohibition?
  5. Sexuality is "language" that says something about God. What does your sexual life (or your chastity) say about God and about who you are?

Key Idea

Key Idea: Sexuality is God's gift, a vocation to love, a visible sign of the immaterial love of God for His people. Living it according to God's plan is living in truth, freedom, and joy.

Cultural Riches

John Paul II, Theology of the Body — Between 1979 and 1984, the Pope gave 129 audiences that form this masterpiece. It is not a handbook of sexual morality, but a meditation on the meaning of the human body. His central thesis: the masculine and feminine body is a "sacrament" (visible sign of invisible reality) of God's love. Required reading for understanding the Church's teaching on sexuality.

The Four Loves by C.S. Lewis — Although not Catholic, Lewis distinguishes with clarity four types of love: storge (affection), philia (friendship), eros (romantic-passionate love), and agape (sacrificial love). Christian love integrates all: eros ordered to agape is true conjugal love. Eros separated from agape is idolatry or consumption.

The Fathers of the Church on Marriage — St. John Chrysostom called marriage "a little royal throne," because spouses are kings and queens of their home, co-operators with God in the creation of new lives. St. Augustine saw in marriage a "sacrament of the union of Christ with the Church." These saints did not despise marriage; they elevated it to a sacred mystery.
