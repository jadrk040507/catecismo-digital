Catechist's Guide — The Liturgy: Encounter with Christ

Catechist's Guide

For class preparation and delivery. For the catechist's use only.

Class Summary

Estimated duration: 60 minutes

Central theme: The liturgy as an encounter with Christ, in whom the Paschal Mystery works through signs and sacraments.

Objectives:

  • Understand that the liturgy is the work of Christ, not a human invention.
  • Discover the Paschal Mystery as the heart of every liturgical celebration.
  • Distinguish between the common and ministerial priesthood.
  • Value liturgical signs and the Liturgical Year.
  • Participate actively in Mass, not as a spectator but as a protagonist.

Key verse: «They devoted themselves to the apostles' teaching and to fellowship, to the breaking of bread and to prayer» (Acts 2:42)

CCC references: 1066-1134, 1085, 1090, 1141-1178

Suggested Class Structure

Section Duration What the catechist does
1. Big Question (Emmaus) 8 min Tell the story of Emmaus (Lk 24:13-35). Ask: «What makes an ordinary gathering become an unforgettable encounter?»
2. What Is the Liturgy? 10 min Explain the meaning, origin of the word, who the true actor is. Activity: who acts at Mass?
3. Paschal Mystery 8 min Explain that Christ's Pasch is made present. Read 1 Cor 5:7.
4. Common & Ministerial Priesthood 7 min Differentiate both priesthoods. Ask: «What is your role at Mass?»
5. Time and Signs 10 min Explain the Liturgical Year, signs. Show a liturgical calendar if possible.
6. For Reflection 10 min Reflection questions in small groups, then share one answer in plenary.
7. Closing: Key Takeaway + Prayer 7 min Read the Key Takeaway. Pray the workbook prayer together. Assign the workbook for the week.

Notes for the Catechist

The liturgy is not a show

Many people (especially young people) stop going to Mass because they «don't like it» or «get bored.» Help them understand that the liturgy does not depend on our taste. Christ is present and acts regardless of whether the choir sings well or the homily is exciting. The question is not «Did I like it?» but «Did I encounter Christ?»

Common priesthood

It is important that the faithful know they are not spectators. Every baptized person has an active role in the liturgy: responding, singing, offering. Ask: «If all the laity were spectators, why does the priest say 'The Lord be with you' and wait for a response?»

The Paschal Mystery

Explain that the Mass is not a repetition of Christ's sacrifice, but its making-present. Christ offered himself once for all (Heb 7:27), and at Mass that one sacrifice is made present. It is not a new sacrifice, but the same one.

Signs and symbols

Young people especially need to understand the signs: why water, why bread, why incense. They are not empty rituals; they are a language that speaks to body and soul. Help them «read» the signs.

Sunday

Remember that Sunday is not «the day of obligation» as a burden, but the Lord's Day, the «little Easter.» It is a gift, not a burden. The Church asks us to attend Sunday Mass because we need it like the bread we break.

Age Adaptation

🌱 Seed (ages 7-12)

Use the image of a family dinner: Mass is God's dinner with his children. Activities: draw the objects used at Mass (chalice, paten, candles), color a simple liturgical calendar. Teach the responses of the Mass.

🌿 Sprouts (ages 13-17)

Connect with their experience: boredom at Mass, difficulty concentrating. Ask: «What movie or concert keeps your attention? What makes an experience captivating? What if Mass is more real than any concert?»

🪴 Root (adults new to the faith)

Many adults come from traditions where liturgy was less structured. Help them understand the value of the liturgy: it is not rigidity, but communion with the universal Church. Explain the key moments of the Mass.

🌳 Tree (formed adults)

Go deeper into the theology of active participation (participatio actuosa) from Vatican II. Relate the liturgy to the Trinity: the Father is glorified, the Son acts, the Holy Spirit unifies. You can quote Romano Guardini, «The Spirit of the Liturgy.»

Difficult Questions

«I get bored at Mass. What do I do?»

This is an honest question. Help the person understand that Mass is not entertainment; it is an encounter. Suggest: read the readings beforehand, focus on something concrete each Sunday, offer the Mass for a specific intention, sing. Boredom often comes from being a spectator instead of a protagonist.

«Why does the Church have so many rules for the liturgy?»

The liturgical rules are not whims; they are meant to ensure that we celebrate the mystery of Christ correctly and that we are in communion with the whole Church. The liturgy is not the property of the priest or the local community; it belongs to the universal Church.

«Why can't I receive Communion if I'm not married in the Church?»

This is a painful but important question. Communion expresses full communion with the Church. It is not about excluding, but about protecting the truth of the sacrament. The catechist must respond with charity and truth, remembering that one can always receive spiritual communion.

«What if I don't feel anything at Mass? Does it still count?»

The liturgy has value in itself, not because of what we feel. Christ acts even when we don't feel it. Feelings come and go; God's grace remains. Many saints went through spiritual dryness at Mass, but they never stopped going.

Catechist's Prayer

Lord Jesus, who make yourself present in every liturgy
and break bread for us,
teach me to celebrate your mysteries with reverence and joy.
May I never grow accustomed to Mass,
may I never live it as routine.
Help me to convey to those who listen to me
that the liturgy is heaven on earth,
the most important encounter of the week.
Amen.
