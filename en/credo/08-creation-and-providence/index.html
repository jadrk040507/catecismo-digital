Creation and Providence

Big Question

If God did not need to create anything, why does everything exist?

It was June 1833. John Henry Newman, a thirty-two-year-old Anglican clergyman, lay in a bed at an inn in Sicily, alone and consumed by fever. He had been traveling through the Mediterranean for months, and a tropical illness had laid him low. His servants, convinced he would die, had already taken his measurements. But Newman did not die. In the delirium of his fever, a phrase repeated itself over and over in his mind: «I shall not die, for he has a work for me.»

When he finally recovered and could begin the journey home to England, his ship was becalmed in the Strait of Bonifacio for three days without wind. There, in the stillness of the sea, Newman took up a pen and wrote a poem that would become one of the most beloved hymns in the world:

«Lead, Kindly Light, amid the encircling gloom, lead Thou me on! The night is dark, and I am far from home; lead Thou me on!»

Newman did not know where he was going. He did not understand why God had saved him from the fever. But he trusted that Someone was guiding him. That trust is the essence of faith in Providence: not having all the answers, but knowing Who holds them. The rest of Newman's life confirmed that certainty. He left Anglicanism, became Catholic, was made a cardinal, and wrote some of the most luminous pages on divine Providence ever penned. He said that God created not out of necessity, but because love —like light— tends to share itself, to pour itself out, to illuminate. Have you ever stopped to think that you exist not because God needed you, but because he loved you before you existed?

Activity

Go outside or look through the window. Choose something you can see — a tree, a cloud, a bird — and say aloud: «God wanted this to exist». Now do the same with yourself. What do you feel when you say «God wanted me to exist»?

Creation, work of the Trinity

Creation is not the work of God the Father alone. It is a Trinitarian work. The Father creates through the Son and in the Holy Spirit. Saint John says it at the beginning of his Gospel: «All things came to be through him, and without him nothing came to be» (Jn 1:3). And Genesis shows us the Spirit of God hovering over the waters (Gn 1:2). Thus, creation bears the mark of the Trinity: the Father who loves, the Son through whom all is created, the Spirit who gives life and order (CCC 290-292).

This matters because it means the world is not something God «threw away». Every created being bears within itself a relationship with the Creator. Creation is an act of communal love: the Father, the Son, and the Holy Spirit share their life and joy with what did not exist. As the Catechism says: «Creation is the foundation of all God's saving plans» (CCC 288).

Connection

Look up Gn 1:1-2; Jn 1:1-3 and Col 1:15-17. Underline the references to the Father, the Son, and the Holy Spirit in creation. How does your vision of the world change if you know that every particle was created by Trinitarian love?

God creates from nothing

The Christian faith professes that God created the world «from nothing» — in Latin, creatio ex nihilo. This is not a scientific theory; it is a theological truth. It means that before creating, there was nothing that was not God. There was no eternal matter alongside him, no chaotic force he had to dominate. God did not shape something that already existed: he drew the entire universe from nothing by his word (CCC 293-295).

«Then God said, 'Let there be light,' and there was light» (Gn 1:3). Creation is an act of pure freedom. God was not obliged to create. He lacked nothing. But he chose to create because love, by its nature, gives itself, shares itself, generates life. As Wisdom says: «For you love all things that are and loathe nothing that you have made; for what you hated, you would not have fashioned» (Wis 11:24) (CCC 295).

Context

In the ancient world, many religions believed that the gods had formed the world from preexistent matter — chaos, water, primordial monsters. The biblical account is radically different: God does not struggle against chaos, he dominates it with his word. He does not mold eternal clay, but calls into existence what did not exist. This doctrine of creatio ex nihilo was explicitly developed in the 2nd and 3rd centuries to defend the absolute freedom and sovereignty of God (CCC 285-289).

The angels and the visible world

Creation encompasses two orders: the visible world (the material) and the invisible (the spiritual). Angels are spiritual beings created by God, «ministers and messengers» of his plan of salvation (CCC 329-330). They have existed from the beginning, are personal and immortal, and have intelligence and will. They are not myths nor pious ornaments; they are real creatures who participate in the history of salvation (CCC 331-333).

The visible world, in turn, is good because God created it and declared it good: «God saw how good it was» (Gn 1:10.12.18.21.25). Matter is not evil nor a trap of the devil; it is God's creation. The human being, created in God's image, is the pinnacle of visible creation: a being who participates in both worlds, the spiritual and the material (CCC 326-327).

Activity

Read Gn 1:31: «God saw everything that he had made, and found it very good». Make a list of 5 beautiful things you can see right now. How does it feel to know that God declared them «very good»?

Divine providence and the problem of evil

If God created everything and is almighty, how is it that evil exists? This is one of the oldest and most painful questions of humanity. The Catechism does not evade the difficulty. It teaches that God governs the world with his providence: «the disposition by which God guides his creation toward perfection» (CCC 302). God is not absent nor indifferent; he is actively conducting creation toward its ultimate end (CCC 303-305).

But providence respects freedom. God created free beings — angels and humans — and true freedom includes the possibility of choosing evil. God permits evil, but he does not will it. And from every evil, God knows how to bring forth a greater good. The definitive proof is the Cross: from the worst crime in history, God brought the greatest salvation (CCC 309-314).

Going Deeper

Saint Thomas Aquinas synthesizes the doctrine of providence with a luminous phrase: «God, in his providence, permits evil in order to bring forth from it a greater good». It is not about justifying suffering, but trusting that God does not lose control. The Resurrection did not cancel the Cross; it transformed it. How can you live that trust in your own life?

Creation is not a past event that God «left running». God sustains creation in every instant. Without his continuous action, everything would return to nothing. «For in him we live and move and have our being» (Acts 17:28). Your existence in this second depends on the creative love of God, not only of the one who acted at the beginning, but of the one who acts now (CCC 301).

Biblical Connection: The poem of creation

The Lord made the earth by his power,
founded the world by his wisdom,
and stretched out the heavens by his understanding.
At his thunder, the waters roar in the sky,
he makes clouds rise from the ends of the earth,
produces the lightning and the rain,
and brings forth the wind from his storehouses.

Jeremiah 10:12-13

The Bible is not a book of science; it is a book of faith. It does not describe how the universe works mechanically, but who created it and why. The creation accounts in Genesis do not compete with physics or biology; they answer a deeper question: where does everything come from, and for what? The answer of faith is clear: it comes from God, and for his glory and our good (CCC 283-289).

For Reflection

  1. What strikes you most about the fact that God created the world «from nothing»? What does it imply for your life that you do not exist by chance?
  2. If creation is the work of the Trinity (Father, Son, and Holy Spirit), how does that change your way of seeing the world around you?
  3. God declared that his creation was «very good» (Gn 1:31). Do you find it hard to believe that the material world is good? Why?
  4. How do you reconcile God's providence with the suffering you see around you? What does the Cross tell you about it?
  5. Look around you right now. Can you see something that reminds you that God loves you and sustains your existence in this instant?

Key Takeaway: God did not create the world out of necessity, but out of love — and he continues to sustain it in every instant.

Cultural Resources

«The Creation of Adam» by Michelangelo (Sistine Chapel) — At the center of the Sistine ceiling, God extends his hand toward Adam, who barely lifts his. The fingers almost touch. That tiny space between them is where everything is at stake: the initiative is God's, who approaches; Adam only has to respond. It is said that one hand is a man's and the other a woman's, symbolizing the maternal and paternal love of God. The image captures something essential of Christian faith: creation is an act of love that seeks response. God does not impose; he invites.

Film «The Tree of Life» (Terrence Malick, 2011) — This film intertwines the story of a family in Texas with grandiose images of creation: galaxies, volcanoes, oceans, dinosaurs. Malick asks: how can we believe in a good God in a world full of pain? The film does not give easy answers, but beautifully shows that all of creation hides the mark of a Creator who loves.

«Canticle of the Creatures» by Saint Francis of Assisi — Composed shortly before his death, this canticle celebrates God through all creatures: «Praise be to you, my Lord, through all your creatures». It is perhaps the oldest ecological poem in the Christian tradition, seeing in every creature a reflection of the Creator. Saint Francis did not worship nature; he worshipped the God who created it.
