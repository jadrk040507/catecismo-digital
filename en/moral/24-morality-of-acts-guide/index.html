Catechist's Guide — The Morality of Human Acts

Catechist's Guide

For preparing and leading the class.

Class Summary

Estimated time: 60 minutes

Core topic: The morality of human acts depends on three sources: the object chosen, the intention, and the circumstances. All three must be good for an act to be morally good.

Objectives:

  • Understand the three sources of morality for human acts.
  • Distinguish between intrinsically evil acts and acts that depend on circumstances.
  • Recognize that a good intention cannot justify an evil act.
  • Understand the principle of double effect and its limits.

Key verse: «The tree is known by its fruit» (Mt 12:33)

Catechism references: CCC 1749-1761

Suggested Class Structure

Section Time What the Catechist Does
1. Big Question 10 min Maria’s story. Ask: «Is her gift good or not? Why?»
2. Three Sources 15 min Explain object, intention, circumstances. Use everyday examples.
3. Knowledge & Will 8 min Invincible vs vincible ignorance. Discuss responsibility to know.
4. Double Effect 10 min Explain the principle with the medical example. Ask for other examples.
5. Biblical Connection 7 min Read Mt 12:33-35 aloud. Discuss.
6. Reflection & Close 10 min Reflection questions. Small group sharing.

Notes for the Catechist

Key teaching: The three-source framework of Catholic morality is one of its greatest strengths. It avoids two extremes: the rigorism that only looks at the external act (object alone), and the relativism that says «only intentions matter».

Intrinsically evil acts: Emphasize that some acts are always wrong. This is not «rigid» but rather protects human dignity. The Church teaches this with confidence because it flows from the natural law, knowable by reason, not just by faith.

The end does not justify the means: This is perhaps the most important moral principle to convey. Our culture constantly says otherwise. Give strong, clear examples.

Age Adaptation

🌱 Seed (Ages 7-12)

  • Simplify: «God sees not just what we do, but why we do it.»
  • Activity: draw a tree with «good fruit» and «bad fruit» actions.
  • Avoid double effect. Focus on the three sources.

🌿 Sprout (Ages 13-17)

  • Connect to their world: social media, peer pressure, «everyone does it».
  • Discuss real scenarios: «Is it ok to cheat if my family needs me to pass?»
  • Double effect as a tool for thinking about complex situations.

🪴 Root (Adults new in faith)

  • May have a legalistic or a permissive background. Help them find the middle.
  • Emphasize that God cares about the whole person, not just rule-keeping.

🌳 Tree (Formed adults)

  • Explore the principle of double effect in bioethics (end-of-life care, pregnancy complications).
  • Compare with consequentialist ethics (Utilitarianism) and why the Church rejects it.

Difficult Questions

1. «Is it always wrong to lie? Even to save a life?»

Yes, the act of lying is intrinsically evil (CCC 2484-2485). But you are not obligated to reveal the truth to someone who has no right to it. You may remain silent, deflect, or state what you have a right to say. The classic example: hiding Jews in your home during the Holocaust. You don’t need to lie — you can say «there is no one here» meaning «no one here who you have a right to find» (mental reservation).

2. «Isn’t the Catholic approach too complicated?»

Moral life is complex because life is complex. The Church gives us principles, not a checklist. The more you form your conscience, the more natural it becomes to discern well. It’s like learning a language: at first every word is hard, but eventually it flows.

3. «What if I didn’t know it was wrong?»

If you truly could not have known (invincible ignorance), you are not culpable. But you have a responsibility to form your conscience. The question is: did you choose not to know?

Catechist’s Prayer

Lord Jesus, Teacher of the moral life,
grant me wisdom to teach the truth with clarity,
and charity to apply it with mercy.
Help those entrusted to me to act with integrity,
choosing what is good, intending what is right,
living each moment for Your glory.
Amen.
