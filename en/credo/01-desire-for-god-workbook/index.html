Workbook — The Desire for God

Student Workbook

For use after class or in groups. Print this page or work in your notebook.

Recalling the essentials

  1. What is the phrase from St. Augustine that opens the lesson? What does it mean?
  2. Explain in your own words Fulton Sheen's image of the «God-shaped hole».
  3. What does the Catechism mean when it states that man is «capable» of God (CCC 27)?
  4. What are the two opposite errors about faith that the lesson points out?

For reflection and writing

Answer each question sincerely. There are no «correct» answers to these personal questions.

1. What things do you seek to feel happy? (friends, success, rest, screens...)

Make a list of 5 things you turn to when you feel empty or lonely.

___________ ___________ ___________ ___________ ___________

2. Has any of those things given you happiness that lasts forever?

If the answer is no, why do you think we keep looking in the same places?

3. Of the seven obstacles to recognizing God (CCC 29), which seems most common in your environment?

(Rebellion against evil, ignorance, indifference, riches/cares, bad example, hostile currents, sin)

4. Have you ever felt that «something more» that you can't quite identify?

Describe the situation: When? Where? What were you doing?

5. What person in your life has helped you seek truth or ask important questions?

The Catechism says that «man needs the witness of others who teach him to seek God» (CCC 30). Who has been that witness for you?

Practical activities

A. The map of the heart

Draw a large heart on a sheet of paper. Divide the heart into sections like puzzle pieces. In each section, write something that occupies space in your heart: your family, your friends, your dreams, your fears, your hobbies... Leave one section empty, unfilled. That section is the «God-shaped hole». Reflect: What are you putting there that isn't God? What would happen if you let God occupy that space?

B. Psalm 42 in first person

Read Psalm 42 slowly. Then write it in your own words, as if you were the one saying to God:

«As the deer longs for water, so my soul longs for you, my God...»

C. Dialogue with Fulton Sheen

Imagine you can talk with Venerable Fulton Sheen. He tells you that you have a God-shaped hole in your soul. What do you respond? Write a brief dialogue (10-12 lines) between you and him.

 

 

 

 

 

 

Verse to memorize

«You have made us for yourself, O Lord, and our heart is restless until it rests in you.»

— St. Augustine, Confessions I,1 (also CCC 30)

Prayer for this week

Lord, I thank you because you made me for yourself.
I recognize that sometimes I fill my heart with things that don't satisfy me.
Help me recognize my deepest desire,
and not be afraid to seek your face.
You who are living water, quench my thirst.
Amen.
