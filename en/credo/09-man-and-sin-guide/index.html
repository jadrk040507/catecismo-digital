Catechist's Guide — Man and Sin

Catechist's Guide

For class preparation and delivery. For the catechist's use only.

Class Summary

Estimated duration: 60 minutes

Central theme: The human being was created in God's image, wounded by original sin, yet God never abandoned us and promised redemption from the very beginning.

Objectives:

  • Understand that the human being is God's image, created for communion.
  • Know the unity of body and soul as an essential part of human dignity.
  • Understand original sin: not mere disobedience, but pride and distrust in God.
  • Recognize the consequences of sin: the wounded nature, concupiscence, broken relationships.
  • Embrace the promise of the Protoevangelium: where sin increased, grace abounded all the more.

Key verse: «Where sin increased, grace abounded all the more» (Rom 5:20)

CCC references: 355-421

Suggested Class Structure

Section Duration What the catechist does
1. Big Question (Saint Augustine) 10 min Tell the story of Saint Augustine in the garden. Ask: «If we were created for good, why do we choose evil?»
2. Created in God's Image / Body and Soul 12 min Explain Gn 1:27 and human dignity. Hands activity (seed). Connection with Psalm 8.
3. Male and Female / The Fall 12 min Dignity and equality. Then the narrative of Gn 3: the serpent, temptation, disobedience. Emphasize that sin is pride, not just weakness.
4. Consequences / Protoevangelium 10 min Explain the wound of original sin and concupiscence (Rom 7:15). Then the promise of Gn 3:15: the Protoevangelium.
5. For Reflection 8 min Invite participants to share one of the 5 reflection questions in small groups, then plenary.
6. Closing: Key Takeaway + Prayer 5 min Read the Key Takeaway. Pray the weekly prayer together, emphasizing «where sin increased, grace abounded all the more.»
7. Week assignment 3 min Hand out the workbook. Invite them to write the letter to God starting with «Lord, I hid myself because...»

Notes for the Catechist

Saint Augustine's story

It is important to narrate Augustine's conversion not as a distant tale, but as something that connects with everyone's experience. Augustine lived sin in his own flesh: the theft of the pears (he didn't need them; he stole for the pleasure of doing evil), his addictions, his search for love in the wrong places. And then grace found him. That is exactly what Romans 5:20 affirms: where sin increased, grace abounded all the more.

The image of God

Do not reduce «image of God» to intelligence or will alone. The Catechism emphasizes the capacity to know and love (CCC 356). We are God's image because we can enter into a relationship of love. This means the image of God is manifested especially in communion: male and female together, in community, not in isolation.

Original sin

A common mistake is to think that original sin was «eating a piece of fruit.» The fruit is symbolic; what is essential is the decision to want to be like God without God. It is the root of all sin: pride, distrust in God's love, the desire for absolute autonomy. Help the group see that the serpent does not force Eve; it tempts her. Temptation always works this way: it distorts God's word and sows doubt about his intentions.

Concupiscence vs. sin

It is crucial to distinguish between original sin (a historical event) and concupiscence (the inward tendency to sin that remains as a consequence). Concupiscence is not sin in itself; it is the inclination. Saint Paul describes it perfectly in Rom 7:15: «I do not do what I want, but I do the very thing I hate.» We all experience that tension.

The Protoevangelium

Gn 3:15 is one of the most important verses in all of Scripture. It is the first promise of salvation, given immediately after the Fall. God does not wait; even in punishment there is hope. The woman whose offspring will crush the serpent's head is Mary, and her offspring is Christ. Connect this with the Annunciation (Lk 1:26-38): Eve said «no» in the garden; Mary said «yes» in Nazareth.

Age Adaptation

🌱 Seed (ages 7-12)

Use the broken mirror metaphor: we are like a mirror that reflects God's image, but it broke. The pieces still reflect the light, but the image is distorted. Jesus comes to repair the mirror. Activities: draw the garden of Eden before and after sin, memorize Rom 5:20 with hand signs, write three things they can do out of love.

🌿 Sprouts (ages 13-17)

Connect with their experience of temptation: «What ‘serpents’ whisper to you today that God doesn't love you?» Social media, peer pressure, the culture of «you decide what's right for you.» The story of the Fall is not a distant myth; it repeats every time we choose our own will over God's love. Compare Gn 3 with the Annunciation: Eve said «no»; Mary said «yes.»

🪴 Root (adults new to the faith)

Many adults carry guilt and shame from their past. Help them see that sin does not have the last word. Saint Augustine's experience is powerful: «I was made and I unmade myself.» But also: grace found him. It's not about denying evil, but about confessing that Christ has already conquered it. The key question is: «In what areas of your life do you need Christ to ‘crush the serpent's head’?»

🌳 Tree (formed adults)

Go deeper into the theology of original sin: the distinction between personal sin and sinful condition, the sense of «sin of the world» in John, the doctrine of solidarity in Adam (monogenism vs. polygenism). Quote the Church Fathers: Irenaeus («recapitulation»), Augustine («mass of sin»), Anselm (satisfaction). The Adam-Christ typology (Rom 5:12-21) is central.

Difficult Questions

«If God is good, why did he create the serpent?»

The serpent is not a creature created by God to tempt man. The Catechism teaches that the devil and the demons were angels created good by God, who freely rebelled against him (CCC 391-395). The serpent is the symbol of Satan, who uses an existing creature to tempt. God did not create evil; evil is a privation of the good, a distortion of what God made good.

«Is it fair that we all bear Adam's sin?»

Original sin is not personal guilt imputed to us. It is a condition, not an act. Saint Paul explains it with the image of solidarity: just as we are united to Adam by nature, we are invited to be united to Christ by grace (Rom 5:12-21). We are not guilty of Adam's sin, but we suffer its consequences, just as a child may suffer the consequences of a parent's decisions without being guilty of them (CCC 404).

«Is the Eden account historical or symbolic?»

The Catechism says that the Genesis account uses «figurative language» to describe a «primo-genital» event that really occurred (CCC 390). That is: the form of the narrative is symbolic (a talking serpent, magical trees), but the event is real: there was a first human pair who sinned freely, and that sin had consequences for all humanity. We do not need to take the account literally to believe that the Fall was real.

«If we are already baptized, why do we keep sinning?»

Baptism erases original sin, but it does not eliminate concupiscence (the tendency to sin). We continue to struggle against that inward tendency. Saint Paul describes it in Rom 7 and offers the solution in Rom 8: the Holy Spirit gives us the strength not to live according to the flesh. Baptism gives us grace, but the ongoing struggle is part of the Christian life. We are not alone: «I can do all things through him who strengthens me» (Phil 4:13).

Catechist's Prayer

Lord, you who created us in your image
and did not grow weary of seeking us when we were lost,
give me a compassionate heart for those who listen to me.
Help me to show that sin does not have the last word,
that your mercy is greater than our frailty,
that where sin increased, grace abounded all the more.
May my words not be condemnation,
but a bridge to your love.
Amen.
