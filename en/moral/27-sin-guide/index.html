Catechist's Guide — Sin

Catechist's Guide

For preparing and leading the class.

Class Summary

Estimated time: 60 minutes

Core topic: Sin is real and breaks our relationship with God and others, but God’s mercy is always greater.

Objectives:

  • Define sin as an act of unloving that wounds our relationship with God.
  • Distinguish between mortal and venial sin.
  • Recognize the capital vices as roots of sin.
  • Convey trust in divine mercy as the central message.

Key verse: «Where sin abounded, grace abounded all the more» (Rom 5:20)

Catechism references: CCC 1846-1876

Suggested Class Structure

Section Time What the Catechist Does
1. Big Question 10 min Bag of money story. Ask: «Have you ever carried something you knew was wrong?»
2. Reality of Sin 10 min Definition, original/actual distinction. Emphasis: sin is unloving.
3. Mortal vs Venial 10 min Explain the 3 conditions. Avoid creating scrupulosity.
4. Capital Vices 8 min Brief list. Ask: «Which do you think is most common today?»
5. Mercy 10 min MOST IMPORTANT part. The prodigal son. Mercy is always greater.
6. Reflection & Close 12 min Reflection questions. Share reconciliation experiences if trust allows.

Notes for the Catechist

Key point: The class on sin can be the heaviest or the most liberating, depending on how you handle it. The emphasis should ALWAYS be on God’s mercy. St. John Paul II said: «There is no sin that God cannot forgive.» Sin is real, but mercy is more real.

Watch out for scrupulosity: Some people, especially the more sensitive, can become scrupulous (seeing mortal sin where there is none). Clarify that all three conditions must be met SIMULTANEOUSLY. Doubt about whether a sin is mortal already indicates it probably is not.

Age Adaptation

🌱 Seed (Ages 7-12)

  • Simplify: «Sin is when we say no to God, like when Peter denied Jesus. But Jesus always forgives.»
  • Don’t go into mortal/venial. Talk about «being naughty with God.»
  • Activity: draw Jesus hugging someone who did something wrong.

🌿 Sprout (Ages 13-17)

  • Connect to their experience: «Are there things you know are wrong but you do them anyway?»
  • Talk about Confession as a second chance, not as punishment.
  • Discuss how our culture minimizes sin («it’s not that bad»).

🪴 Root (Adults new in faith)

  • May carry shame from the past. Emphasis that God has already forgiven them.
  • Help them make their first Confession if they haven’t.

🌳 Tree (Formed adults)

  • Develop the theology of sin: «aversio a Deo et conversio ad creaturam» (St. Thomas).
  • Discuss the relationship between personal sin and social/structural sin.
  • Theological controversy about hell (CCC 1033-1037).

Difficult Questions

1. «Can God forgive any sin?»

Yes. There is no sin God cannot forgive. The only «unforgivable» sin is blasphemy against the Holy Spirit (Mk 3:29), which the Fathers interpret as the deliberate refusal to accept God’s mercy. God always forgives, but He respects our freedom to reject that forgiveness.

2. «How do I know if a sin is mortal?»

If you had serious doubt while doing it, it probably wasn’t. Consult it in Confession. God is more merciful than your scruples.

3. «Are non-Catholics condemned?»

The Church teaches that those who through no fault of their own do not know the Gospel but seek God with a sincere heart can be saved (CCC 847-848). Salvation is possible for all, but the Church is the ordinary path.

4. «Are there sins that cry to heaven?»

Yes, four: willful murder, sodomy, oppression of the poor, and defrauding workers of their wages (CCC 1867). All can be forgiven, but their gravity is such that they cry out for justice.

Catechist’s Prayer

Merciful Lord,
You who know the heart of every person,
help me speak of sin with truth and love.
May I not sow guilt where You sow hope.
May I not minimize evil where You call to conversion.
May I point to the wound while also pointing to the Healer.
Amen.
